BOUNDED BLAST COSMETICS — macOS / Metal

StagedExplosion exposes takeCosmeticEvent after its first published physical
change: a nonzero existing-grain impulse or an accepted terrain batch. It returns
one centre/radius/sequence descriptor per command. Taking it again or committing
later terrain batches cannot emit another event. Preparation, rejection and zero
energy do not emit. Cancellation retains an unconsumed event for effects already
published; destroying the command discards it. No visual allocation is required
for a physical publication to succeed.

planBlastCosmetics creates bounded visual-only seeds from that event. Defaults
are 48 dust and 16 sparks; hard limits are independently 128 and 64. Dust has
0.5..2 m/s initial speed, .055 m radius and .8..2 s lifetime. Sparks have 3..8 m/s
initial speed, .018 m radius and .2..0.8 s lifetime. Directions cover the upper
hemisphere; centres start within half the blast radius. These visual seeds may
extend outside the basin or intersect terrain. They do not represent physical
matter and never authorize terrain removal, heat, mass or energy changes.

Each visual family has an independent SplitMix64 stream keyed by command sequence.
Changing one quota cannot change the other's seed sequence. Integer random bits
are portable; final trigonometric floating-point results are reproducible on the
same host/library, not promised bit-identical across platforms. Planning has no
physical pool handles and cannot consume physical capacity. Lifetime is always
finite. Invalid centres, radius and quotas reject before creating output.

GpuBlastCosmetics consumes the seeds into separate GPU partitions: the preview
uses 512 dust slots and 256 spark slots. Capacity is configurable up to 2048 per
family (4096 total); a family can be disabled with zero slots. A single bounded
allocation dispatch scans each partition in ascending slot order. Full or
exhausted-generation slots cannot steal capacity from the other family. Dropped
counts saturate at UINT32_MAX. Existing effects are never overwritten on overflow.
The API accepts no physical pool, material table or terrain handles. Visual seed
records have zero inverse mass and never enter the physical solver.

GPU evolution uses fixed 1/60 s steps. Dust has upward acceleration .4 m/s^2 and
drag 1.8/s; sparks have downward acceleration 9.81 m/s^2 and drag .3/s. There is
no collision or heat transfer. Expiration retires slots; reuse advances generation
without wrapping. The render stream fades alpha over the finite lifetime. A soft
circular fragment shader uses alpha blending, depth testing and no depth writes.
Cosmetics draw after opaque terrain/particles; particles are not depth sorted,
so overlapping transparent effects are approximate.

The preview consumes one event after accepted physical publication. Visual
allocation failures report a skipped burst without cancelling the physical
command. Cosmetic time continues through terrain preparation/backpressure and
stops when the user pauses. The Blast cosmetics panel reports active and dropped
counts per family, and provides Clear cosmetics. Resetting the particle pool,
rebuilding terrain or switching diagnostic modes clears effects and counters.
Cancellation retains already emitted effects, which expire normally. Diagnostics
read four count words asynchronously; production never reads full visual state.

Validation
Real Metal tests exercise disabled families, workgroup boundaries, maximum
capacity, exact seed upload, independent overflow, retained drop counters,
CPU-referenced trajectories, render-instance alpha, expiration, generation reuse,
exhausted generations, invalid input and reset. An offscreen pixel test verifies
visible output, approximately half brightness at half lifetime and black output
after expiration. A nonzero physical pool remains byte-identical throughout.
The prior event tests retain acknowledgement, once-only and cancellation guards.

Verification limit: the live preview and the previously installed baseline both
showed blank native windows in the current desktop session. Process sampling
showed ongoing frame submission and Metal fallback drawable allocation. Raising
and resizing did not restore presentation. The cause is not established. Live
control/appearance QA must be repeated when window presentation is available;
passing offscreen pixel tests do not close that check.
