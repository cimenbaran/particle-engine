PRIVATE GPU DEPOSITION CLAIMS — macOS / Metal

GpuDepositClaims runs the owner/target preflight, then resolves a bounded private
claim set. It never changes terrain, source particles, settling history or mass
ledgers. Capacity is 0..4096 slots and quota 0..512 voxels (default 32). All inputs
must remain immutable/alive through execution; a later transaction must freeze
and validate their owner, material encoding, terrain revision and source tick,
and reject latched base-physics faults before publication.

Arbitration visits candidate owners in ascending stable-slot order. A ready,
unclaimed target chooses the nearest unclaimed same-material eligible partner
within .1875 m per axis of its cell centre. Equal float distances use the lower
slot. Previously rejected owners may become partners of later targets. A voxel
or source grain can appear in at most one accepted claim. New terrain is not
used as support until a later acknowledged transaction.

The proposed voxel must clear every remaining solid sphere. Owners selected by
previous accepted claims are excluded. Exact common-radius face tangency is
preserved by comparisons against exactly representable separating planes. Other
edge/corner distances use a conservative uncertainty band: squared radius times
1.00001 plus 1e-10 square metres. Ambiguous contacts defer; they never authorize
penetration. Together with conservative candidate-speed eligibility, this can
produce fewer claims than the double CPU reference near numeric boundaries.
Float nearest-distance rounding may also resolve almost-ties differently from
the CPU reference. No cross-device bit-identical replay is promised.

Each pair has already passed the material-specific two-grain/one-voxel mass
check. The output contains two uvec4 lanes per claim: signed voxel x/y/z/material,
then first slot/generation and second slot/generation. Unused rows have UINT_MAX
sentinels. A private uint ownership buffer stores zero or one-based claim index
per source slot; this is a proposal, not live retirement. Four status words give
errors, accepted voxel claims, claimed grains and clearance-deferred proposals.
Any preflight error yields zero claims and a cleared ownership buffer.

The initial resolver uses one deterministic invocation and bounded scans, with
at most six storage bindings. It prioritizes correctness and is not evidence of
the final throughput target. Worst-case scan cost and a more parallel arbitration
path remain part of performance work before expanding the live pool. Production
has no source-particle readback in this primitive; full readbacks are test-only.

Validation compares exact fixture claims against the CPU reference, independently
checks ownership, empty targets, long-double per-material mass and remaining-
sphere clearance, and compares source/history bytes before and after. Cases cover
workgroup boundaries, 4096 slots, quotas including zero/512, mixed materials,
fault rollback, mass-tolerance rejection, blocked proposals, exact tangency,
one-ULP penetration, nearest-partner ties, conservative corner deferral and repeat
submission. Claim packing also tests negative voxel keys across chunk seams.

Remaining: compact asynchronous claim acknowledgement, candidate occupancy/mesh
publication, generation-qualified retirement only after publication, deposit mass
ledgers, wake handling and live controls. This is not live deposition yet.
