LIVE STAGED TERRAIN EXPLOSIONS — macOS / Metal

Open --terrain-preview, collapse Terrain editing if more panel space is needed,
and expand Explosion. Choose Blast centre, Blast radius, Debris speed and Terrain
energy, then Start blast. A new command snapshots these settings; changing the
sliders does not change an in-flight command. The preview converts up to 32
voxels per accepted batch. Existing-particle blast impulses and cosmetic blast
emissions remain open; the current energy budget funds terrain conversion and
its new physical debris only.

The preview uses calibrated fracture resistance in J/m^3: Sand 100, Wood 300,
Ash 50, other materials 1000. Density, solid phase and fuel come from the loaded
material table. The coordinator checks that energy-profile density and phase
match the physical material table exactly; a lower-density budget cannot fund
heavier GPU grains. Persisting resistance in material JSON is still pending.
See Terrain_Explosion.txt for radial selection, placement and energy arithmetic.

Coordinator and ordering
StagedExplosion owns the immutable request, material profiles, remaining budget,
owner identity and expected terrain publication. pump runs before simulation and
rendering. It submits one PendingDebrisTransaction at the late preparation view,
then waits for both GPU acknowledgements. A successful early-view publication
adopts terrain and grains first, charges the downward-rounded remaining budget,
and records committed batch/voxel/grain totals. The same call never prepares the
next batch. At least one simulation tick must pass before the next preparation.

A pending batch freezes simulation through the existing transaction lease.
A paused scene may finish an already submitted batch; it does not start another
at that same tick. Pending particle commands run before the next preparation.
This lets users clear or move blocking grains without a blast overtaking their
queued command. At most one batch is attempted at a tick, with fixed CPU scan,
voxel and product quotas. The UI bounds radius to two metres; the API supports
up to four metres under its scan limit.

Capacity and particle-overlap rejections leave terrain, particles and energy
unchanged. The coordinator releases the freeze and retries only after 30 new
simulation ticks. Paused commands therefore do not spin expensive preparations.
The panel reports whether it is waiting for capacity or debris clearance.
Clearing the blocking material, or letting it move away, allows later batches
to proceed. The deterministic placement does not search alternate particle-free
sites on the GPU; a settled obstruction can keep a blast deferred indefinitely
until the user changes particle space or cancels it. No terrain is discarded to
force progress. A CPU placement failure stops with NoSpace. Other validation or
physics faults stop the command instead of retrying endlessly.

Empty terrain, an unaffordable remainder or an impossible product quota ends the
command with a distinct reason. Cancel discards only a pending batch; already
published edits and their charges remain. Reset/rebuild and switching diagnostic
modes cancel the command before replacing the pool. Different terrain or particle
owner identities invalidate a command even when revision/tick numbers happen to
match. Terminal commands release retained world resources. Another terrain edit
cannot be started from the preview while a blast is active.

Validation
Seven real Metal coordinator scenarios cover three automatic one-voxel batches,
no pre-ACK energy charge, freeze and same-tick/paused gates, cancellation before
and after publication, full 1024-slot capacity rejection and recovery, a bounded
30-tick retry, terrain/pool replacement, density/phase mismatch, exhausted energy,
impossible quota, queued command ordering, latched physics faults and buried
placement failure. The existing twelve transaction scenarios and planner tests
remain in the full suite.
