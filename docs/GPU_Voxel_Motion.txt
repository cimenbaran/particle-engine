GPU VOXEL MOTION AND BASIN COLLISION PROBES

GpuVoxelMotion predicts and responds to solid particles against GpuVoxelWorld
occupancy. It consumes the standard 64-byte particle records after forces have
been applied. Each successful pass changes only position and velocity xyz.
Inactive particles, non-solid phases and static bodies are preserved. The source
store, world revision and shared origin must match before dispatch.

The bounded profile is dt in (0,.01] seconds, a speed cap in [.001,12] m/s, a
nonnegative bounce threshold, and 1..64 impact events (default 16). Subnormal dt
is rejected. The maximum capped displacement is .12 m, within the shared short
sweep's .125 m per-axis range. The pass allocates three 64-byte-per-slot state
buffers and 16 bytes of status; capacity zero uses one slot of storage. Submit
allocates nothing and does not read back. Caller buffers must contain capacity
complete records. Output alternates when fed back as the input.

The finite resident box acts as a closed boundary, inset by particle radius.
Each event clips the candidate segment to the next boundary plane, then tests
all candidate occupied voxels before that plane. Earliest voxel contact is
resolved first. Non-entering contacts/tangencies are excluded only in the motion
query; the public geometric query retains its closed-contact semantics. After
a response, that convex voxel is excluded from further entry events until a
different collider changes velocity. An outgoing straight ray cannot re-enter
it; initial-overlap and final-containment checks are still performed.
Terrain restitution is the minimum of the particle and terrain coefficients;
friction is their geometric mean. A single impact uses the normal velocity change
to bound tangential friction. Resident boundary planes retain the particle's
coefficients. Speeds below the bounce threshold have no restitution response.

A 2 micrometre normal separation is preferred after impact, but is checked
against all nearby occupancy. In a tight gap that offset may enter another
voxel. The fallback retreats along the already swept path, doubling from 2 to
256 micrometres; failure to find a contained point is an arithmetic error.
A retreated impact consumes only the corresponding fraction of travel time,
preserving motion along an unconstrained tangent for the rest of the substep.
No unchecked normal offset or unbounded retreat is published. Small rounded
endpoint penetrations (at most 2 micrometres) use the same checked repair without
an extra velocity impulse. Larger penetration fails the pass.

Earlier contacted voxels remain active while within a 4 micrometre separation
shell at the new geometric contact point. Up to eight active contacts are retained, with overflow treated as an
error rather than silently dropping constraints. For multiple active normals,
velocity is projected into their non-entering cone: test the input, projections
onto each plane, intersections of each pair of planes, and zero, choosing the
closest feasible vector. This prevents repeated restitution events from failing
in nearly closed wedges. The numerical velocity feasibility tolerance is
5e-7 * max(speed,.001). This is contact response, not sleeping or a speed cutoff.

Status words: error mask, proposed speed caps, completed collision events and
incident normals. Error bits are 1 upstream, 2 invalid particle/material or initial
overlap, 4 event/contact budget exhaustion, 8 arithmetic or containment failure.
Any slot error causes every output slot to copy the immutable input. Counters
on failed passes describe partial proposals, not committed motion.

The basin inspection panel now has Drop collision probes and Pause probes.
VoxelProbes owns 256 Sand records, seeded over the ledge and slope. Four 1/240 s
substeps per 60 Hz tick apply existing GPU gravity/drag and the voxel response.
A latched error rolls back the entire tick, including age. Asynchronous status
readback pauses the diagnostic run and reports a fault. Re-dropping resets the
probes; rebuilding the basin clears them. Probe rendering uses existing particle
impostors. The normal particle sandbox still uses its original granular solver.

These probes do not interact with one another and are not the physical command
pool. They provide runtime terrain-response validation, not deposition, debris,
settling certification, mass-accounted editing or full granular integration.
The next integration must constrain pair-solver position corrections against
terrain, finish velocities against terrain contacts, and extend the grid and
support/settling diagnostics to the full resident region.

Validation: 22 motion fixtures / 1,748 particles use an independent double
response reference plus an analytic narrow-wedge free-axis check. They check input immutability, unchanged metadata,
energy non-increase, separation, material combinations, walls, corner events,
speed caps, zero capacity, and whole-pass rollback. The wedge case requires
nonzero velocity and correct displacement along its unconstrained Z direction. The live basin test advances
256 probes for 600 ticks / 2,400 substeps (10 simulated seconds), checks identity,
age and final occupancy separation including probes below the ledge, rejects a
stale world before mutation, then injects an overlapping edit and verifies exact
whole-tick rollback. Existing geometry and renderer tests remain in the suite.

The seeded slope test initially exposed two failures: at about tick 49 a skin
crossed a neighboring voxel; after a first offset fix, about tick 72 repeated
wedge contacts exhausted the event budget. The checked separation and contact
cone resolve those cases with the same event limit and penetration tolerance.
A later free-axis fixture caught lost tangential travel during backoff, and
restoring that time exposed impossible repeated entries on the same convex
voxel. Time accounting and event eligibility now have regression coverage.
This short correctness run does not replace the planned long terrain/edit soak.
