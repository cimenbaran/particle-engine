PRIVATE GPU BLAST IMPULSES — macOS / Metal

GpuBlastImpulse prepares velocity changes for existing solid particles without
modifying the source. PendingBlastImpulse now freezes the owner, validates an
asynchronous acknowledgement and adopts the result while invalidating settling
history. StagedExplosion uses it once before terrain conversion with an explicit
shared-budget allocation. See Pending_Blast_Impulse.txt and Staged_Explosion.txt.

Supported profile
Capacity is 0..1,048,576 slots. Request centre lies in the basin, radius is
.0625..4 metres, radial velocity increment is 0..12 m/s and energy is either zero
or 1e-6..1e9 joules. The request is immutable and submit is single-use. Materials
must have stable IDs and known phases. Dead particles, nonsolids, static particles
with zero inverse mass, and centres outside/on the radius remain unchanged.
Solid input position/radius/velocity lanes must be finite; radius is (0,1], each
position coordinate is within one million metres, inverse mass is zero or
[1e-6,1e6], and squared input speed is at most 144.001. This tolerance admits
rounding around the existing 12 m/s profile. Bad live ownership/generation,
material identity or numeric data rejects the entire candidate.

Velocity proposal
Use quadratic radial falloff at the current centre position. Within one micrometre
of the blast centre, use a deterministic upward direction. Add the radial vector
to the old velocity and cap the result to 11.99998 m/s, leaving a binary32 margin
below the 12 m/s physical limit. A zero requested speed is an exact no-op.
Only the velocity xyz components may change. Position/radius, inverse mass,
thermal state, material, flags, stable slot and generation are copied unchanged.

Energy allocation
Let d be the magnitude of the actual encoded velocity change, v the old speed,
and m the mass from the particle's actual inverse mass. A conservative bound on
positive kinetic-energy increase is m * (v*d + .5*d*d). This accounts for existing
motion; using only .5*m*d*d would underestimate work on an already moving grain.
Inflate the binary32 bound by 1.00001 and add 1e-12 J to cover arithmetic rounding
and small-vector underflow in the validated domain. Braking never credits energy
to other recipients: its positive-work bound is still charged conservatively.

The host converts the supplied budget into at most 16,777,215 integer tickets.
A binary32 joules-per-ticket value is rounded down so its double-precision product
with that ticket count cannot exceed the original budget. Each candidate costs
ceil(bound / joulesPerTicket) + 1 tickets. A candidate too expensive for the
whole budget uses the budget-plus-one sentinel. Integer prefix sums saturate at
UINT32_MAX, never wrap. Apply only a contiguous affordable prefix of candidate
costs in ascending stable-slot order; unaffected zero-cost slots are skipped.
An unaffordable candidate stops allocation to later candidates, even if a later
one is cheaper. This deterministic prefix policy is not a fairness optimizer.

The accepted ticket sum is bounded by the ticket budget. Multiplying the returned
uint24 ticket count by the binary32 quantum is exact in binary64. It is a reserved
upper bound on positive work, not a measurement of exact energy added. Quantized
reservation may leave energy unused or conservatively overcharge small effects,
especially with very large budgets. It must come from a separate existing-grain
allocation; never reuse an amount already reserved for debris or fracture.

GPU graph and status
Clear four status words, propose immutable candidates and costs, scan costs with
saturating arithmetic, then apply accepted velocities to a private full-state
output. A global error makes output byte-equivalent to the original and leaves
accepted count and ticket charge zero. All allocations occur during construction;
submit uses ordered dispatches and at most six storage bindings. Production code
has no particle readback in this primitive.

Status words are: error bits (1 identity, 2 numeric, 4 material), eligible count,
accepted count and tickets charged. Source storage must cover capacity particle records; material storage must cover
the supplied material count. The caller retains source/material buffers
through execution and must not mutate them between proposal and acceptance.
The output/status handles are owned by the one-shot operation.

Validation
235 real Metal batches inspect 1,553,038 full particle slots. Cases cover workgroup
boundaries and a million-slot allocation whose integer sum would overflow a
wrapping scan. A double-precision radial oracle checks accepted velocities. The
sum of positive before/after kinetic-energy changes must not exceed the charged
energy or original request. All non-velocity lanes and the full source are checked
byte-for-byte. 208 random batches span every combination of 16 energy magnitudes
and 13 speed settings, varied masses, old velocities, dead/static/nonsolid slots.
Explicit cases cover centre/near-centre directions, exact/adjacent radius bounds,
braking, the speed cap, mass endpoints, generation UINT32_MAX, zero/tiny/large
budgets, malformed state rollback, invalid host input and repeated submission.
