RESIDENT VOXEL VELOCITY FINISH

GpuVoxelMotion::finish is a post-pair-solve constraint pass. It uses the same
immutable world revision/owner/origin contract and ping-pong storage as swept
prediction. A caller supplies the corrected/reconstructed particle state and
an external upstream status. The object's own status cannot be upstream.
Any error copies the complete input, preserving whole-pass rollback.

Positions, radius, inverse mass, identity and thermal state stay bitwise fixed.
Dead, non-solid and infinite-mass slots retain the existing exclusion policy.
Dynamic solids must be contained and strictly nonpenetrating. A 4-micrometre
contact shell collects nearby occupied voxels and resident boundary planes.
The shell spans at most three voxel cells per axis: 27 candidates plus at most
three resident planes fit in 32 entries, with explicit overflow failure. Every
nearby occupied material must be a valid solid with friction in [0,1].

The solve projects velocity into the feasible contact cone. Nonzero solutions
in 3D need at most two independent active normals; candidates are tested against
all collected normals. The retained tangent is selected by its speed, avoiding
cancellation when a large normal impulse dwarfs a small valid sliding velocity.
Single-plane projections use cross products to keep residual error proportional
to the retained tangent. Nonnegative normal impulse multipliers determine the
friction budget: sum(mu_i * lambda_i). Each voxel coefficient is the geometric
mean of particle and terrain friction; a resident plane uses particle friction.
Exact ties retain deterministic traversal order. Zero velocity is a feasible
fallback when no nonzero candidate satisfies all constraints.

Friction scales the common tangent without reversing it. Separating velocities
receive no new impulse or friction. No restitution is added here: prediction
already resolved impacts. The speed limit is applied after contact response,
so a large inward component does not first scale away legitimate free sliding.
Power-of-two preconditioning keeps arithmetic finite for huge finite inputs.
Numerical feasibility permits residual inward speed up to 1e-5 m/s at the final
check; exceeding it rolls back the pass. This is an inelastic finishing stage,
not a new impact prediction or a static-traction budget.

Status words
[0] 1 upstream failure, 2 invalid state/material/overlap, 4 contact capacity,
    8 arithmetic/constraint failure.
[1] Bodies capped after contact response.
[2] Bodies whose velocity changed, including speed caps.
[3] Collected contact-shell normals. Other words are provisional on failure.
The buffers remain GPU-resident; synchronous exports occur only in tests.

Tests compare an independent double active-set/Gram solve and analytic cases,
including six faces, rounded corners, perpendicular surfaces, a narrow wedge,
mixed voxel/wall friction, tiny free tangents, huge normal velocities, zero
friction, shell exclusion, speed limits, energy, repeated finishing, exact
position preservation, stale context rejection and mixed-pool rollback.
A seeded 256-body corner batch runs with friction and without it.
See ../tests/results/m3-max-voxel-finish.txt for execution results.

Remaining integration
The live basin still needs its full force/prediction/pair/correction/response/
finish coordinator, terrain static traction, and support/settling tracking.
This pass does not enable grain-to-grain interaction in the probe UI yet.
