BOUNDED TERRAIN EXPLOSION PLANNING — macOS development

TerrainExplosion.hpp provides a read-only, typed ExplosionRequest and a plan
that can be passed to PendingDebrisTransaction. StagedExplosion now owns live
batched execution and command energy in the preview; see Staged_Explosion.txt.
Existing-particle blast impulses and cosmetic blast quotas remain open.

Request and bounds
The request contains tick, stable sequence, centre, radius, maximum debris speed
(impulse), energy in joules, voxel quota and product grain quota. Validation
requires the current tick, a basin centre, radius (0,4] metres, speed [0,12] m/s,
energy [0,1e9] J, 1..512 voxels and at most 4096 product grains. CPU material
profiles supply density, solid phase, fuel and positive calibrated fracture
resistance in J/m^3 (at most 1e12). Resistance is an explicit planner input; it
is not yet persisted in the material JSON/GPU ABI. The GPU fixture uses 1000
J/m^3 for every material; CPU fixtures exercise different strengths.

Selection and energy model
Enumerate voxel centres in the blast's clipped basin bounding box, at most 65^3
checks. Sparse store lookups exclude nonresident/empty cells. Select centres
strictly inside the sphere; boundary falloff is zero. Order by distance, then
higher Y, lower Z, lower X for deterministic ties. The attenuation is

    f = (1 - distance / radius)^2
    fracture charge = resistance * voxel volume / f

This is a calibrated work model, not a pressure-wave/fracture solver. A resistant
voxel that cannot fit the energy budget remains intact; cheaper later candidates
may still be selected. Recompute each material's mass-quantized grain count as
voxels are added. Never exceed either batch quota. Every additional grain reserves
0.5 * encoded grain mass * maximum speed^2 of kinetic energy. Positive arithmetic
is rounded upward for the charged budget, including accumulation. The returned
reservedJoules is the authoritative conservative charge; the fracture/kinetic
components are explanatory diagnostics. A boundary-sized budget may need the
small rounding guard, not just a hand-computed mathematical value.

All products use the shared TerrainDebrisPlacement.hpp lattice placement from
excavation: one grain per removal plus nearby free sites within .5 m per axis,
complete per-material mass accounting and no duplicate sites. A local placement
failure returns NoSpace, no removals or grains and zero reserved energy. It does
not remove a smaller arbitrary subset or silently discard required products.
The planner's deterministic placement is not an exhaustive packing solver.

Give each placed grain an outward radial velocity at its own attenuated distance.
The exact centre uses the upward direction. A site outside the blast has zero
initial velocity. Component rounding is reduced toward zero when necessary so
the encoded vector never exceeds its allocated speed. This remains below the
conservative kinetic reservation even when placement moves extra grains away
from source voxel centres. Temperature is 293.15 K and fuel follows the profile.
The planner does not read current particle state; the GPU clearance gate remains
mandatory for both existing-particle and candidate-terrain validation.

Commit and continuation contract
A proposal does not mutate terrain or consume energy. Submit its removals and
seeds through PendingDebrisTransaction using the matching source revision/tick.
Only after successful joint publication may a caller subtract reservedJoules
from the remaining command budget. Use explosionEnergyRemainder for downward-
rounded subtraction so repeated batches cannot create rounding energy. This
math helper does not authorize publication. A rejected, cancelled or stale batch must
leave that budget unchanged. Replan against the newly published terrain and
remaining energy for the next bounded batch; unconverted voxels stay intact.
Do not fund existing-particle impulses or other physical effects from the same
already reserved energy. The live coordinator schedules at most one bounded batch per tick. Extending
it to existing-particle impulses still requires an explicit budget partition.

Validation
150 deterministic CPU plans check radial membership, resistance, capacity,
energy boundaries, encoded mass, outward velocity, immutable retries, sparse
residency, malformed input, buried placement failure and the actual basin.
A 49-voxel fixture applies seven acknowledged-model batches of seven voxels,
keeping all other terrain intact and charging only accepted batches. This is a
CPU staging fixture, not proof of a live automatic explosion scheduler.
The real Metal transaction fixture converts 32 basin voxels to 61 radial grains,
measures initial kinetic energy from complete GPU records, then checks all
61 grains through 240 physics ticks. Existing excavation regressions retain
138 planner cases and the live-compatible repeated transaction tests.
