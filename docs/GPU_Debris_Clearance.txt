PRIVATE DEBRIS CLEARANCE — macOS / Metal

GpuDebrisClearance is a read-only geometric gate for a private activated debris
batch. It checks new grains against all indexed solids, including other new
grains, and against the candidate terrain occupancy. Existing-existing overlap
is left to the solver. A batch can proceed only when the aggregate error is zero.
This gate neither adopts particle state nor commits terrain removal.

Submission follows GpuDebrisActivation and the candidate occupancy upload in a
sequential view or a later view. Across frames, prior work must have completed.
The activation, its borrowed reservation claims, the material table and terrain
must remain alive and unchanged until clearance completes. The coordinator must
validate the material epoch and base tick. The CPU rejects unsubmitted or wrong-
capacity activation, invalid/aliased material buffers, invalid material counts,
and stale, foreign or differently based terrain before dispatch.

Pipeline
1. Build a fresh Basin spatial index of the activated state. Validate the common
   .0625-metre radius globally for every indexed solid, not just nearby ones.
2. Check activation success, counts, ascending claims and matching slot/generation
   identities. An activation failure prevents claim reads even if requests exceed
   capacity. Validate each new material remains solid.
3. For each new grain, traverse all solids in its 27 neighboring .125-metre cells,
   excluding only its own slot. There is no neighbor truncation. Static solids
   also block placement; dead and nonsolid particles are excluded by the index.
4. Reject strict sphere overlap; exact tangency is clear. The shared compensated
   comparison lifts both endpoints before subtraction to retain signed subnormal
   coordinates. Spatial cell selection also preserves their sign.
5. Query the candidate voxel occupancy at each new position. Contact and miss are
   clear; overlap, invalid queries and footprints outside residency reject.
6. Reduce per-request errors and counts. No CPU readback or allocation occurs
   during submit. Consumers can use the existing asynchronous 16-byte diagnostics.

Aggregate words: error OR, requested count, clear count, rejected count.
Error bits: 1 failed activation/count, 2 malformed claim/identity/material,
4 spatial index or radius profile, 8 particle overlap, 16 terrain rejection.
Errors may combine. Zero-request batches still validate activation/index status.
Per-request records use three 16-byte lanes: pose, uint claim, and uint fields
(error, visited other particles, hash probes, terrain query kind).
Only rows below requested count are meaningful. Outputs may be reused by the
next submission; inputs and live state are never modified.

Limits
Capacity is inherited from the existing spatial primitives (up to 1,048,576);
activation accepts at most 4,096 seeds. This gate has at most six compute-buffer
bindings per pass. Dense regions are fully traversed and may be expensive;
performance acceptance is separate from correctness. Caller-owned material
hashes and snapshot provenance cannot be proved by a raw GPU buffer handle.

PendingDebrisTransaction now freezes the base tick, verifies per-material mass
plans and stable materials, acknowledges preparation and jointly adopts terrain,
particles and conversion totals. See GPU_Debris_Transaction.txt. Failed private
outputs are discarded. Live edit/explosion controls and deposition remain open.

The GPU fixture runs 59 batches and 6,332 queries over capacities
0,1,127,128,129,255,256,257,4096. It includes a 4,096-seed batch, a single query
visiting 4,095 neighbors, CPU all-pairs comparisons, new-new and new-existing
pairs, exact/inward/outward tangency, signed subnormals on both endpoints,
static solids, ignored dead/nonsolid particles, invalid radius, failed activation,
malformed claims, voxel tangency/overlap/removal and residency rejection.
Readback confirms activated and live particle buffers remain byte-identical.
Host fixtures reject stale/foreign/displaced occupancy, unsubmitted activation,
capacity mismatch and invalid/aliased material inputs. The shared comparison
also retains the existing 37,042 spawn-clearance query regressions.
