ACKNOWLEDGED EXISTING-GRAIN BLASTS — macOS / Metal

PendingBlastImpulse wraps the private GpuBlastImpulse kernel. Construction
validates the command pool, full material encoding and zero-origin terrain, then
freezes the owner through the shared transaction lease. Submit prepares a private
particle candidate and requests one 16-byte acknowledgement combining impulse
status with latched simulation health. Production code reads no particle array.

Before publication, the candidate cannot change live particle bytes or consume
energy. Poll checks errors, counts and ticket limits. Ready exposes a proposed
charge; only successful publish authorizes the caller to subtract it. Publish
checks the exact owner, lease, source handle, tick, material hash and terrain
publication pointer. Replaced owners or same-revision replacement terrain make
the result stale. Rejected, stale and abandoned operations do not spend energy.
Destruction drains outstanding acknowledgement reads before releasing resources.

A nonzero accepted count swaps the private output into the current particle
state, rebuilds pool membership, clears settling history and starts diagnostic
epochs. Terrain and conversion mass totals remain unchanged. A zero-effect
publication preserves the state handle, settling history and diagnostic epochs.
Queued particle commands remain pending during the freeze and run afterwards.

StagedExplosion reserves a configurable maximum share for this phase, once before
terrain conversion. It subtracts the published ticket charge using downward
remaining-budget arithmetic. All unspent energy can then fund terrain/debris.
Later terrain batches cannot kick existing grains again. Cancellation preserves
already published effects and their charges. See Staged_Explosion.txt.

Validation
Six real Metal scenarios verify immutable preparation, owner freeze, competing
operations, invalid views, single adoption, exact non-velocity bytes, kinetic
energy bounded by the charge, explicit clearing of injected settling history,
queued removal, zero conversion-mass changes, malformed generations, latched
health faults, stale terrain/pools, abandonment and altered material encoding.
The zero-budget case checks unchanged particle storage and retained injected
settling history. Ten coordinator scenarios include shared impulse/terrain
budgets, three subsequent terrain batches and cancellation after the impulse.
