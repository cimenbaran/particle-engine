TERRAIN / GRAIN MASS QUANTIZATION

The common granular radius is .0625 m and the voxel edge is .125 m. At equal
material density, a spherical grain has pi/6 of one voxel's mass (about .523599).
A one-grain-per-voxel conversion would lose about 47.64% on destruction and gain
about 90.99% on deposition. It is not an acceptable hidden approximation.

The initial conversion mass profile retains density-derived spherical grain
mass and chooses the nearest whole product count separately for each material.
The maximum permitted absolute mass difference is 5% of that material's source
mass per proposed conversion. This is an explicit quantization approximation,
not exact conservation. A caller may tighten the limit down to zero, but may
not increase it above 5%. Errors of unlike materials must never cancel to make
an otherwise invalid conversion appear acceptable.

A one-voxel debris proposal requires two grains and reports roughly +4.72% mass.
Two matching grains deposit into one voxel with roughly -4.51% relative to their
source mass. A single grain cannot deposit alone. Larger material batches use
the closest integer product count, generally reducing relative error. Inputs
which exceed the tolerance remain pending with their original physical state.
Capacity or placement failure is a separate reason to defer the entire batch.

TerrainMass.hpp exposes pure proposal calculations:
- conversionMassProfile(density) computes the float inverse mass activation must
  upload, its represented mass, and the voxel mass.
- planVoxelDebris(voxels,density,tolerance) returns the closest grain count.
- planGrainDeposit(grains,density,tolerance) returns the closest voxel count.
Each balance includes both counts, encoded inverse mass, source/destination kg,
signed quantization kg, relative error and the mass-tolerance decision. Positive
quantization means added represented mass; negative means lost represented mass.
Rejected proposals still report their closest count and difference for diagnostics.

Density must match the material schema (.001 through 100000 kg/m3). Depositing
by count requires every input grain to have exactly this material and encoded
inverse mass. Arbitrary masses or another material profile require measured-mass
planning instead. Phase, material identity/hash, voxel claims, particle ownership,
spatial clearance and reservation are validation requirements of the later
transaction coordinator, not of this numeric helper. Counts are uint32; an
unrepresentable debris product count throws before narrowing. Empty conversions
have zero mass and can satisfy zero tolerance.

Numeric accounting
Grain mass is derived from sphere volume and density, then encoded as binary32
inverse mass for ParticleGpu. Accounting uses that stored reciprocal, avoiding a
second independently rounded mass during activation. The representation error
relative to the density-derived sphere is below 7e-8 in the tested profile.
Density times inverse mass divided by 512 is an exact binary64 factor because
both inputs are binary32. Fused multiply-add resolves half-count boundaries
without discarding low product bits. Exact count ties round upward.

The signed mass difference is computed from the compensated count residual,
not by subtracting two large rounded mass totals. This preserves tiny nonzero
differences under a zero-tolerance policy. Reported totals reconcile within
floating-point rounding. A matched voxel-to-grains-to-voxel round trip restores
the original voxel count and cancels the signed differences exactly; unrelated
conversions must still accumulate their actual signed differences.

Transaction integration
These are proposed balances only. They do not alter terrain or particles and
do not update a committed mass ledger. The coordinator must reserve slots,
validate debris placement/initialization, wait for required GPU acknowledgements,
and then publish both terrain and particle state at one tick boundary. Only
successful commits may update per-material source/destination/quantization
counters. Cancelled and capacity-deferred proposals must not change those totals.
PendingDebrisTransaction now implements these gates and committed conversion
counters for debris (GPU_Debris_Transaction.txt). Edit/explosion controls,
deposition, whole-world inventory and UI diagnostics remain pending.

Tests use a long-double nearest-mass oracle over 128,282 balances, plus exact
integer-rational boundary fixtures independently derived from binary32 values.
They cover schema density extremes, resident-scale and large counts, both signs,
tight/zero tolerances, matched round trips, invalid inputs and count overflow.
One regression at density 2700 and 253,061,881 voxels distinguishes the correct
483,312,608 grains from the naive double-rounding result of 483,312,609.
